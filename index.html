House rules
  1. File at least one report a week through this terminal. Two silent weeks marks you missing.
  2. Delivery runs follow your sponsor's roster. If you can't make one, say so here in advance.
  3. Stakes are held and returned by your sponsor, in person. No member handles another member's stake.
  4. Survey sheets stay on this terminal. No screenshots, no forwarding, no coordinates in group chats.
  5. Questions about the books go to a Warden. Members don't settle accounts between themselves.
  6. Don't recruit. If you meet someone promising, tell your sponsor. The Wardens decide.
  7. On public servers, never speak in the Concord's name. No shared skins, no group photos.
  8. The Concord's history, roster, and server locations: to outsiders, you don't know.
  9. Loans between members are logged in items, seven-day term. Overdue goes to your sponsor.
  10. To leave, tell your sponsor a week ahead, return your survey copies. Your stake comes back in full, in person.
  11. Two missed tasks without notice drops you to intake. Once more during intake, you're out.
  12. Anything this code doesn't cover: ask your sponsor first, then a Warden. Don't guess.

About

The short version

We don't run a server of our own. We play on servers you've heard of — and we end up near the top of them.

The Concord has worked the same way since 2014: join an established world, map it, learn its economy, and build influence until the server quietly runs on our infrastructure. Shops, farms, roads, stashes — eventually, everyone trades with us whether they know it or not.

Some servers call it tryharding. We call it Tuesday. Every few worlds we take on one or two new people, usually players we've actually spent time with. That's still how it works.

Operations

How a server gets taken over

Economy

Shops, auction flips, bulk farms. Control the supply of two or three key items and the market adjusts to you.

Intelligence

Coordinates, maps, seed notes. We know where things are before most players finish their first base.

Infrastructure

Roads, hubs, public farms. The things everyone uses and nobody maintains — until maintaining them means owning them.

Joining

How people usually get in

There's no application form. Members bring people in — someone you've played with vouches for you, and you take it from there. If you were given a code for this page, that part already happened. The rest goes like this:

  1. Talk to your sponsor. The member who gave you the code will ask a few questions about the server you're on. Nothing formal.
  2. The stake. New members leave a materials stake with their sponsor — some diamonds or equivalent. It's an old habit from servers where new accounts would occasionally take the gear and vanish. The stake is held, not spent, and it comes back to you in full once you're in, plus whatever kit we can spare.
  3. Hang around. Play with us for a week or two. Run a delivery, finish a survey sheet, whatever needs doing.
  4. That's it. Your sponsor returns the stake, hands over your kit. The rest, your sponsor shows you.

We don't sell spots. If someone claims they're selling Concord membership, they're not with us.
